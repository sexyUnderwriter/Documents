# E. A. Coburn: provisional client and family profile

Research updated: November 2026.

## Finding in brief

**Edward Alvin Coburn, born September 28, 1843, is now a substantially better-supported candidate for the "Edw. A. Coburn" who commissioned 1411 Church Street in October 1910. Contemporary sources confirm Edward A. Coburn and Edward Warner Coburn at 1702 Wesley Avenue. The full identification and title connection are not yet proved.**

A 1913 family genealogy names his wife, Marzetta Helen Coburn, and two sons, Edward Warner and Eliot Alvin. The original **1900 census** places Edward, Marzette H. and their Illinois-born son Eliot A. together in Lowell, Massachusetts; Edward is described as a mirror manufacturer. The 1909-10 Evanston directory places a mirror manufacturer and wife "Marzette" at 1702 Wesley, although its printed initials conflict with the genealogy. Original pages of the **1910 and 1911 Chicago Blue Books name Mr. and Mrs. Edward A. Coburn and Edward Warner Coburn at that same address**. The original 1910 census also lists an Edward A. and Marzette at 1702 Wesley, followed by separate entries for an Edward A., age 29, and an Edward W., age 31. Their relationship to one another remains unresolved.

A [marriage index](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:N7CX-X5V) records **Edward Warner Coburn and Lucile Barry marrying on September 30, 1911 in Evanston**, about eleven months after the permit. The original marriage record has not been inspected. The timing supports the family account, but no record yet proves the couple lived at 1411 or that the house was built for their marriage.

Recorded deed images describe **1702 Wesley and 1411 Church as adjoining portions of Lot 25, Block 3**. They support the subdivision lead but do not establish when the original lot was divided or prove that Coburn held both parcels. The early title chains remain open.

The owner's account that the neighboring homes came from one original property, and that 1411 was built for Coburn's son upon marriage, is retained as a research lead rather than dismissed or presented as established history.

## 2. The candidate family

Silas Roger Coburn and George Augustus Gordon's *Genealogy of the Descendants of Edward Colburn/Coburn* (Lowell, Massachusetts: W. Coburn, 1913), entry 953, printed pages 315-316, records:

| Person | Genealogy's information | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Edward Alvin Coburn** | Born September 28, 1843; son of Alvin Coburn; married June 10, 1874 | Name fits Edward A.; he would have been 67 when the permit was issued |
| **Marzetta Helen Coburn** | Wife; born in Dracut September 3, 1845; daughter of Peter Sullivan and Lucy (Boardman) Coburn | Distinctive first name resembles the Evanston directory's "Marzette" |
| **Edward Warner Coburn** | Son; born January 13, 1879 | Would have been 31 in October 1910; a possible subject of the marriage-house account |
| **Eliot Alvin Coburn** | Son; born January 5, 1883; died December 18, 1904 | Within this candidate family, cannot be the son marrying for a newly commissioned 1910 house |

Sources: original printed [page 315](https://archive.org/download/genealogyofdesce00cobu/page/n374.jpg) and [page 316](https://archive.org/download/genealogyofdesce00cobu/page/n375.jpg); [complete volume](https://archive.org/details/genealogyofdesce00cobu). Both page images were visually checked.

This is a published family genealogy, not a civil vital-record certificate. Entry 953 does not state Edward Alvin's birthplace, occupation, residence in Evanston, death date, or any connection to 1411 Church. The organ-building biography higher on page 315 and the insurance biography lower on page 316 belong to other entries, not Edward Alvin.

An [Ancestry birth-certificate index](https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/2545/records/232391) lists an "Edward Warner Coburne," born January 13, 1879 in Chicago, with father "Edward Alvin Coburne" and mother "Magetta Clinton Coburne" (FHL film 1287800). The names and date make this a valuable lead, but the indexed mother conflicts with the genealogy's **Marzetta Helen**; a [second birth index](https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/2545/records/1570358) has another maternal transcription. Inspect the original certificate before reconciling the entries or treating the connection as proved.

## 3. Family connections and source conflicts

### 1900: a Lowell household with matching family and trade

The [original 1900 census](https://archive.org/download/12thcensusofpopu661unit/page/n470.jpg), enumerated June 8, lists a Coburn household at **35 Varnum Avenue, Lowell, Massachusetts**:

| Line | Person | Details recorded |
|---|---|---|
| 37 | Edward Coburn, head | Born September 1842, age 57; **mirror manufacturer**. The index reads his middle initial as A, but later marks obscure it on the image. |
| 38 | Marzette H., wife | Born September 1844, age 55; married 26 years; two children born and two living |
| 39 | Florence B. Marshall | Sister-in-law |
| 40 | Eliot A. Coburn | Son, born January 1883 in Illinois, age 17; at school |

Citation: 1900 U.S. census, Massachusetts, Middlesex County, Lowell Ward 7, ED 817, sheet 9A, dwelling 176, household 180, lines 37-40; NARA roll T623_661. The [FamilySearch index](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:M9TW-X9R) provides an alternate entry point but misstates the ages on the image as 58 and 56.

The wife's distinctive name and Eliot's name and birth month, year and state closely match the 1913 genealogy; the mirror trade also resembles the 1909-10 Evanston listing. The older son Edward Warner is not listed in this particular household, which does not establish where he was then. The image gives **1842 and 1844** as the parents' birth years, one year earlier than the genealogy's **1843 and 1845**. Their ages on the 1910 census fit the genealogy's years; obtain vital records before resolving the discrepancy.

### 1909-10: the Wesley Avenue household

Printed page 140 of the Evanston directory reads:

> Coburn E W (Marzette wife) mnfr mirrors r 1702 Wesley av tel 632
>
> Coburn M H jeweler Chas Graves & Co Chicago r 1702 Wesley av tel 632

Source: [original directory page 140](https://archive.org/download/evanstondirector119091910evan/page/n127.jpg), visually checked.

**Those initials are in the original print; they are not simply OCR mistakes.** The wife-name points toward Edward Alvin's household, but "E W" matches the son's initials, while "M H" matches the mother's initials. The Blue Book evidence below makes a compilation or printing error more likely, although the exact correction remains unproved.

The 1900 Lowell census independently associates the mirror trade with a strong candidate for the elder Edward, making that reading of the Evanston directory more plausible. It does not justify silently changing the directory's printed initials. Nor should the "M H" jewelry occupation be silently reassigned to his son. The print identifies a household and occupations but contains unresolved identity conflicts.

### 1910-11: independent confirmation at 1702 Wesley

The **1910 Chicago Blue Book**, printed page **750**, under Wesley Avenue, reads:

> 1702 Mr. & Mrs. Edward A. Coburn
>
> 1702 Edward Warner Coburn

The **1911 volume**, printed page **775**, repeats both entries. Its alphabetical list, printed page **556**, separately lists Edward A. and Edward W. Coburn at 1702 Wesley.

Sources, all visually checked: [1910 page 750](https://archive.org/download/chicagobluebooko1910chic/page/n763.jpg), [1911 page 775](https://archive.org/download/chicagobluebooko1911chic/page/n788.jpg), and [1911 page 556](https://archive.org/download/chicagobluebooko1911chic/page/n567.jpg). The Wesley heading for the 1911 continuation is on [page 774](https://archive.org/download/chicagobluebooko1911chic/page/n787.jpg).

This confirms **Edward A.**, rather than merely an ambiguous E. W., at the Wesley address during the house-commission period, together with **Edward Warner**. Combined with the distinctive wife-name in the city directory and the genealogy's father/son names, the evidence strongly favors the candidate family. The Blue Book does not itself call Warner their son, expand Alvin, name Mrs. Coburn, prove ownership, or establish an exact move or marriage date. These are two annual editions of one publication, not two wholly independent publishers.

### 1910 census: a household and an ownership clue

The [original census sheet](https://archive.org/download/13thcensus1910po240unit/page/n746.jpg), enumerated April 20, 1910, gives **1702 Wesley Avenue**, household 153. Its relevant lines read:

| Line | Name and age | Relationship and occupation as recorded |
|---|---|---|
| 24 | Edward A. Coburn, 66 | Head; merchant in plate glass |
| 25 | Marzette, 64 | Wife; two children born, one living |
| 26 | Edward A., 29 | Son; jeweler |
| 27 | Edward W., 31 | Relationship field blank; occupation "none" |

The sheet reports the home as **owned free of mortgage**. This is a household report, not a deed or proof that the head owned either particular parcel. A servant is also listed.

The two younger entries really do appear on **separate lines of the original**, not just in different online transcriptions. Whether they represent different people, a duplicate, or another enumerator error is unresolved; the sheet does not explicitly identify Edward W. as a son. The 1913 genealogy names Edward Warner and Eliot Alvin as the couple's sons and says Eliot died in 1904. That is consistent with the Lowell census report of two children living in 1900 and Marzette's 1910 report of one living child, but it does not by itself explain the two 1910 entries. Warner's age matches the genealogy, while the jeweler occupation resembles later directory entries for him; neither observation proves how to reconcile the lines.

Citation: 1910 U.S. census, Illinois, Cook County, Evanston Ward 2, ED 100, sheet 8A, dwelling 116, household 153, lines 24-28; NARA roll T624_240. The [FamilySearch index for Edward W.](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:MKH2-2BP) and [Ancestry entry for Edward A., age 29](https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/7884/records/111899808) identify different lines of this same sheet; FHL film 1374253, FamilySearch digital folder 004970751, image 749.

## 4. Testing the family-house account

Two propositions require separate evidence.

**One original property:** The 1910 permit and later deed images connect **1411 Church and 1702 Wesley to adjoining portions of Lot 25, Block 3**. This is meaningful support for a division of the original lot, but it does not establish that both properties, or the western neighbor, were once in a single Coburn-owned estate. The [February 1928 survey](./IMG_0072.JPG) depicts land west of 1411 and a 16-foot public alley farther west. Reconstruction must account for that land, the alley, original plat boundaries, and full deed descriptions.

**A house for a son marrying:** The 1911 marriage index supplies a plausible date and identifies Lucile Barry as the indexed bride. The 1912-13 directory places E. W. and Lucille at 1225 Forest Avenue. Neither record establishes a house gift, residence at 1411, or the reason for the commission. The family genealogy's entry does not name Edward Warner's wife. The hypothesis remains plausible but unverified.

The earlier house report's description of Coburn as a probable speculative builder/investor has been withdrawn. Failure to find an exact directory name is not evidence of speculative development.

## 5. Highest-value next records

1. **Cook County tract books and deeds for both portions of Lot 25, Block 3.** Work backward from the 1973 Church deed and 1975 Wesley deed, then forward through all parcel splits and conveyances. See the [tract-book research brief](./1411-Church-Street-History.md#5-open-questions-and-where-to-look-next) for legal descriptions, names, document numbers and access. Search Vandervoort/Vandervoorth and Howell as well as Coburn without treating directory occupancy as ownership. Some pre-1985 images are online, but neither PIN nor name results are a comprehensive early chain.
2. **Edward Warner's birth certificate and other family records.** Compare them with the now-inspected 1900 and 1910 census sheets, investigate the two younger entries in 1910 and the conflicting parental birth years, and resolve the birth index's maternal name.
3. **Edward Warner and Lucile's original marriage record and announcements.** Request FHL film 1030508 / digital folder 004271254 / image 1182 at a FamilySearch center or affiliate library. The [Wilmette FamilySearch Center](https://locations.familysearch.org/en/us/il/wilmette/2727-lake-ave) is at 2727 Lake Avenue; confirm hours and image access before visiting. Search the original September 1911 articles and Evanston newspapers through the [Evanston Public Library](https://epl.org/evanston-history-resources/).
4. **1912-13 Church Street listings.** Determine whether the image-only directory includes a reverse occupant listing for this address; the house's entry has not been established.
5. **Obituaries and probate for Edward Alvin and Marzetta Helen.** These may identify the surviving son, addresses, business interests, and real estate.
6. **Contemporary construction notices or White's job records.** Once client identity is firmer, search both Edward A. and Warner variants for a commission description or intended occupant.

### Safe wording for now

> In October 1910, Edward A. Coburn applied to build a residence at 1411 Church Street, naming Charles E. White Jr. as architect. Contemporary social directories place Edward A. Coburn and Edward Warner Coburn at nearby 1702 Wesley Avenue in 1910 and 1911. Their names, combined with other family evidence, strongly favor identifying the applicant as Edward Alvin Coburn (born 1843). A marriage index records Edward Warner Coburn and Lucile Barry marrying in Evanston on September 30, 1911. Later deed images describe the two addresses as adjoining portions of Lot 25, Block 3. A family account associates 1411 with the son's marriage, but the applicant's full identity, historical common ownership, the couple's occupancy, and the purpose of the commission remain to be confirmed.
