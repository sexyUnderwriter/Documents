## 2. Timeline of owners and occupants

### 1909–1910 · A West Evanston block
The 1909–10 Evanston Directory has no entry for 1411 Church; that absence alone does not establish that the lot was vacant. The 1400 block already had houses at 1414, 1415, 1419, 1425, 1427 Church; the Johnsons (an expressman's family) and McDermotts (a fireman and a tailor) lived across the street — a working/middle‑class block at the western edge of town. A short walk east at 1330 Church lived **Peter Christian Lutkin**, founding dean of Northwestern's School of Music (directory: "dean N W School of Music").

### Oct 20, 1910 · Building permit — Edward A. Coburn, owner; **Charles E. White Jr., architect**
- **Permit No. 4176 / Application No. 4219** (IMG_0025, 0026, 0031): a "2‑story, basement and attic frame residence," 30 ft × 26½ ft, 30 ft high, 7 rooms, 1 bath, 2 water closets, furnace heat, gas and electric light, estimated cost **$3,500**. Owner **Edw. A. Coburn**; architect **Chas. E. White Jr.**; builder/carpenter name partly illegible (reads approximately "Jno. L. Strahs").
- **Edward A. Coburn's full identity remains under investigation.** A strong candidate is **Edward Alvin Coburn (born Sept 28, 1843)**, husband of **Marzetta Helen Coburn** and father of **Edward Warner Coburn (born Jan 13, 1879)**, identified in a 1913 family genealogy. An [original 1900 census](https://archive.org/download/12thcensusofpopu661unit/page/n470.jpg) lists Edward and Marzette H. with son Eliot A. in Lowell, Massachusetts, and identifies Edward as a mirror manufacturer. Original 1910 and 1911 *Chicago Blue Book* pages list Edward A. and Edward Warner at 1702 Wesley. The [original 1910 census](https://archive.org/download/13thcensus1910po240unit/page/n746.jpg) lists Edward A., age 66, and Marzette there, followed by distinct entries for Edward A., 29, and Edward W., 31; whether the latter two entries represent different people or an error is unresolved. The 1909–10 directory's printed initials and the 1900 census birth years also conflict with other records. The earlier speculative-builder/investor interpretation is withdrawn: absence of an exact directory name does not establish his motive. See the [provisional Coburn profile](./EA-Coburn-Profile.md).
- **Marriage-house lead:** A [FamilySearch marriage index](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:N7CX-X5V) records Edward Warner Coburn and **Lucile Barry** marrying in Evanston on **September 30, 1911**, about eleven months after the permit. This fits the family account chronologically, but the original marriage record, the reason for the commission, and the couple's occupancy of 1411 remain unverified. A 1912–13 directory instead lists E. W. and Lucille at 1225 Forest Avenue.
- **1702 Wesley land connection:** Deed images describe 1702 (PIN 10‑13‑224‑025‑0000) as **Lot 25 except its West 100 feet**; 1411 is the **East 50 feet of that West 100 feet**. The described parcels adjoin, with Wesley to the east. This does not prove that Coburn held both or establish when the lot was divided. The [Coburn profile](./EA-Coburn-Profile.md) contains the Wesley deed trail and its gaps.
- **The architect is almost certainly Charles Elmer White Jr. (1876–1936)**, the Prairie School architect who came to Chicago in 1903 to work in Frank Lloyd Wright's Oak Park studio alongside Walter Burley Griffin and Marion Mahony, opened his own Oak Park practice in 1905, co‑designed the River Forest Tennis Club with Wright (1906), and was extremely busy in 1909–1913 (15 projects in the 1909 Chicago Architectural Catalogue; Cheney Mansion, 1913). He later formed White & Weber (1922). The EHC permit supplies a direct contemporary attribution for this modest $3,500 project. A comprehensive search of published catalogues has not been completed, so the house's absence from them cannot be asserted. (Wikipedia: [Charles E. White Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_E._White_Jr.); [Wednesday Journal, 2017](https://www.oakpark.com/2017/08/29/estate-home-pedigree/).)
- The 1961 Cook County assessor card gives the building an "age 48," i.e., a 1913 base year, and Realtor.com lists "built 1913." The permit establishes authorization in October 1910, not the start or completion of construction; the completion year remains unverified.

### 1912–1913 · *Gap*
The 1912–13 directory exists on archive.org (item `05743071`) as an image ZIP with no OCR, but individual pages can be retrieved. Its [Coburn entries on printed p. 142](https://archive.org/download/05743071/05743071.zip/05743071%2F05743071_0137.jpg) list E. W. Coburn (Lucille), salesman with Charles E. Graves & Co., at 1225 Forest Ave. This does not establish whether the couple briefly occupied 1411 earlier. No 1912–13 occupant listing for 1411 has been verified; early occupancy remains a gap.

### c. 1914–1916 · **Frederick and Edna von Schlegell**
- 1914–15 Evanston Directory: *"Von Schlegell Frederick (Edna) sales mngr Allis‑Chalmers Co Chgo r 1411 Church tel 632"*; the household also had a live‑in maid, *"Preis Mary Miss maid r 1411 Church."* The [von Schlegell entry](https://archive.org/download/evanstondirecto3_191415evan/page/n634.jpg) was visually checked.
- Frederick von Schlegell (b. Jan 27 1874, Missouri – d. Feb 16 1951, Los Angeles Co., CA) and Edna Means Glover von Schlegell (1875, Ohio – Apr 13 1936, San Gabriel, CA); both buried San Gabriel Cemetery. He was a sales manager for the Allis‑Chalmers heavy‑machinery company's Chicago office. ([Find a Grave 59872052](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/59872052/frederick-von_schlegell), [59872069](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/59872069/edna_means-von_schlegell))
- They are the earliest occupants documented in the sources checked so far. Whether they owned or rented is unknown.

### c. 1917–1921 · **William Swift Lord and Katherine (Catherine) Lois Bigelow Lord**
- 1917–18 directory: *"Lord [Wm] Swift (Catherine) r 1411 Church tel 4462"*; 1920–21 directory: *"Lord Wm Swift (Katherine) r 1411 Church tel 4462"* (also in the street index). The EHC index card (IMG_0041) records the same for 1918 and 1920.
- William Swift Lord (b. Mar 5 1884, Lawrence, MA – d. Nov 16 1960, Evanston), son of Edwin and Julia (Bennett) Lord, married Katherine Lois Bigelow (b. Jul 3 1890, Saugerties, NY – d. Feb 1990, "artist & teacher") on Jan 21 1914. Sons: William Swift Jr. (1915), **John Bigelow (1917)** and **Richard Bennett (1920)** — the latter two likely born while the family lived at 1411 Church — and Donald Ogden (1923). ([Bigelow Society genealogy](http://bigelowsociety.com/rod9/kat97283.htm))
- **Double L puzzle makers:** Swift and Katharine/Katherine Lord made hand-cut wooden jigsaw puzzles sold as **Double L**. [Bob Armstrong's specialist puzzle collection](https://www.oldpuzzles.com/node/1916) dates their Evanston production to the 1930s and reports, citing their granddaughter, that Swift cut the puzzles and Katharine supplied artwork for many of them. A [family account at the Sands Mechanical Museum](http://sandsmuseum.com/misc/jigsaw/jig.html) independently identifies the couple with the brand.
- **Contemporary occupational evidence:** Mary Beimfohr's “Puzzling the Public,” *Profitable Hobbies*, May 1950, available as a [museum-hosted transcription](http://sandsmuseum.com/misc/jigsaw/hobbies.html), describes Swift as a construction engineer during World War II. It places an earlier puzzle-making period in the 1930s and his return to the craft after medically advised retirement in July 1948. In 1950 he worked in a room at his wife's studio and coordinated other cutters. These are later-life occupations, not yet established for his 1917–21 residence.
- **Production at this house remains a lead:** The current owner reports that the Lords made Double L puzzles while living at 1411, pointing to [this Reddit discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/Jigsawpuzzles/comments/18f3fxb/double_l_puzzle_by_swift_katherine_lord_seeking/). The thread could not be retrieved during this check. The accessible sources establish the couple's puzzle-making identity, but neither places production at 1411; their stated 1930s chronology is later than the documented residence here. A dated box label, advertisement, workshop record, or evidence of earlier production is needed to resolve that discrepancy.
- No occupation is printed for Swift in the residence directories. Despite the surname, there is **no evidence** linking him to Lord's Department Store (625 Davis St.), whose ad appears directly beneath his listing in 1920.
- Neighbors in 1920–22 included Edwin C. Austin at 1415 Church and Peter C. Lutkin at 1330.

### 1922–1923 · **Charles H. Poole (Grace), patent attorney**
- 1922–23 directory: *"Poole Chas H (Grace) pat atty h1411 Church"* and street index *"1411 Chas H Poole"*. The "h" identifies a house or home listing; it does **not** by itself establish ownership. EHC card (IMG_0041) lists "1922‑D Poole, Charles H. (Grace) – patent attorney."
- A "Poole C H, lawyer, 1302 Marquette Bldg, r 1631 Hinman av" appears in 1917–18 — plausibly the same man before moving to Church St.
- The dates of the Pooles' arrival and departure are not established by this listing; Simpson appears in the 1928 survey.

## 4. How the house has changed

- **1910**: Permit authorized a 2‑story frame residence with basement and attic, 7 rooms, 1 bath + 2 WCs, furnace, gas *and* electric lighting. The completion date is unverified.
- **1928**: Brick 1‑car garage authorized (G. F. Knight, mason); a garage appears in the 1946 listing. Its relation to the alley needs verification.
- **1946**: Screened porch; sewer/water service work; 1 bath.
- **1953**: Oil burner installed (Becker).
- **1961–62**: Water‑heater relief valve and fire‑resistant ceiling over furnace added under city order.
- **1965**: Exterior repainted and second outlet added in NE bedroom under city order; house was in dark paint; sun porch off first floor; basement toilet.
- **1965–69**: Converted from oil to gas forced‑air heat.
- **1972–75**: Sun porch now called a "den"; window A/C unit; "expandable attic."
- **2008–2013**: New roof, kitchen appliances, bath remodel, 200‑amp service (Coffey/Woltkamp).
- **2013–present**: Second full bath now on the assessor's record (was 1 bath through 1975); exterior red/white/navy; original porch and X‑pattern balcony railing retained.
